# Adaptive course lifecycle

Use this workflow when enrolling, resuming, or revising a persistent course. The
course is a living route around actual learner evidence. It is not a batch job
that generates every lesson before teaching begins.

## Enroll from evidence

1. Identify the capability the learner wants and the conditions under which it
   should work.
2. Read existing profile, relevant attempts, active courses, and next-step notes
   for the established learner ID. Never copy example records into real state.
3. Locate the field, prerequisites, and useful source backbone. Record what was
   inspected and what remains assumed.
4. Create chapters and topics at the resolution needed to see dependencies.
   Mark uncertain later branches provisional.
5. Select one current topic. Give it an observable outcome, teacher, subject,
   skill routes, sources, planned exercise IDs, and a precise next step.
6. Create the learner course workspace and enroll the validated plan only when
   local tracking is wanted. Without an established learner or consent to local
   tracking, keep the plan in conversation.

Enrollment needs a defensible route, not detailed content for every chapter.
Author the first lesson only after the current reasoning gap is clear enough to
choose its explanation and representation.

## Prepare one teaching turn

Before continuing a course:

1. Resolve the selected canonical plan and verify its enrollment fingerprint.
2. Read the current topic, its prerequisites, relevant concept evidence, the
   last next step, and pending course questions.
3. Use one short retrieval or contrast only when it distinguishes a real
   uncertainty. Do not restart from chapter one because time passed.
4. Decide whether the current topic still serves the learner's destination.
   Keep it, repair a prerequisite, or revise the future route explicitly.
5. Load only the lead teacher, subject guidance, and skill routes needed for the
   current lesson.

## Author and teach gradually

Create one coherent lesson around the present reasoning target. Its blocks may
mix text, bullets, equations, code, voice animation, diagrams, simulations, and
exercises when each reveals something different. Keep terms, symbols, examples,
and color meanings consistent across them.

Teach through chat. Publish only finished lesson and artifact snapshots. The
existence of a polished artifact is not evidence that the learner understood it.
When media is unavailable or unnecessary, continue with the representation that
best exposes the idea.

## Update from actual responses

Record at meaningful evidence changes: an attempt, corrected misconception,
independent explanation, delayed retrieval, topic transition, or session pause.
Never invent a response to fill an exercise or progress display.

After recording evidence:

- derive concept, topic, and chapter views from stored events;
- write the exact unresolved question or next move;
- decide whether the next planned topic remains appropriate;
- revise only what the new evidence justifies;
- keep earlier attempts and stable IDs.

Planning states and evidence states answer different questions. `current`,
`planned`, `provisional`, `retired`, and `out-of-scope` describe the route.
`exposed`, `practicing`, `demonstrated`, `retained`, and derived `needs-repair`
describe observed work. Do not convert one vocabulary into the other.

## Revise without rewriting history

A revision may rename a topic, add a missing bridge, reorder future topics,
change a resource, or retire material outside the goal. Increment the revision
once and record the reason. Preserve any identifier referenced by learner events,
lessons, artifacts, exercises, or questions.

If the learner changes the destination, say which part of the old route remains
useful and which part becomes retired or out of scope. Completion records the
learner's course decision; untaught and untested topics retain those labels.

## Resume naturally

Begin from the saved next step and a concrete piece of prior evidence:

> Last time you computed the gradient correctly; the unresolved part was why a
> negative dot product predicts a local decrease.

Use that wording only when the record supports it. If history is missing or
ambiguous, state the uncertainty and ask one targeted question. Personalization
comes from continuity and precision, not invented familiarity or generic praise.
