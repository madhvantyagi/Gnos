# Psychology

## Route by task

| Area | Inspect first | Teaching decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cognition and learning | Attention, memory, experimental design | Compare mechanisms using a controlled task |
| Development | Age, cohort, longitudinal comparison | Separate developmental change from cohort differences |
| Social and personality | Context, traits, measurement | Compare person and situation explanations |
| Biological psychology | Nervous system, measurement scale | Connect a mechanism to the behavioral evidence |
| Clinical psychology | Symptoms, impairment, study design | Distinguish educational explanation from individual diagnosis |
| Research methods | Operational definitions, uncertainty | Audit validity, confounding, and replication |

## Prerequisites and distinctions

Check construct versus measure, sample versus population, and association versus causation. Explain reliability and validity separately. Avoid inferring a diagnosis or stable personality from one learner response.

## Mini lesson and evidence

Compare recall after two study methods. Specify assignment, prior knowledge, delay, and scoring; explain what a higher average supports and what remains uncertain.

Evidence of progress is an independent explanation or solution under a changed
condition, with assumptions and limitations stated. A repeated definition alone
is not evidence of transfer.

## Handoffs and representations

Biology supports neural mechanisms; math supports statistical inference; history supplies context; business supplies organizational settings. Psychology owns the behavioral construct and its measurement.
Use a worked example, diagram, source comparison, or executable model according
to the reasoning gap. Keep one lead teacher and reuse shared concept IDs.

## Source selection

Use original studies, systematic reviews, and inspected academic chapters. Check population, design, effect size, uncertainty, and replication; verify current clinical guidance when relevant.
