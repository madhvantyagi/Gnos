# Psychology

Identify the construct, its measure, the population, and the comparison.
Distinguish reliability from validity, a sample from its population, and
association from causation. Do not infer a stable trait or diagnosis from one
response.

## Ways to show the idea

Use experiment diagrams, timelines, and measured distributions. Use
[Excalidraw](../../lesson-design/references/excalidraw.md) for study structure and
[Manim](../../manim-voice-animation/SKILL.md) when stimulus order or timing
needs narration. Audio or images may be the stimulus itself. A simulation can
show sampling, measurement error, or confounding under stated assumptions.
Connect the displayed result to what was actually measured.

Psychology owns behavioral interpretation; biology supports a mechanism and
math supports inference. No psychology teacher is assigned by default.

## Teaching each area

Choose the matching section. Its order suggests how to build the topic; it is
not a complete syllabus. Course design uses the starting point and source
checks. Lesson design chooses the views that explain the difficult steps.
Treat the named confusion as a possibility, not a diagnosis of this learner.

### Cognition and learning

- **Build:** Start with a familiar task and what is actually measured. Better
  performance on one task need not establish a general capacity.
- **Research:** Use an academic chapter for the proposed mechanism and the original task
  protocol for the finding. Inspect timing, scoring, comparison, and replication
  evidence.
- **Show and check:** A stimulus sequence shows timing. An experiment diagram shows
  comparison. Response distributions show variation. Compare a changed delay or task.

### Development

- **Build:** Start with a measured change across ages or observations. Age, cohort, and
  period can produce different explanations.
- **Research:** Inspect a developmental study with its sampling and follow-up design.
  Check age, cohort, attrition, and whether comparisons follow the same people.
- **Show and check:** A timeline distinguishes study designs. Trajectories show repeated
  observations. A cohort table makes comparisons explicit.

### Social and personality

- **Build:** Start with a behavior in a stated situation and how it was measured. One
  response does not establish a stable trait or isolate a situation's effect.
- **Research:** Inspect the measure, study design, and replication or review evidence.
  Check situation, population, effect size, and variation within groups.
- **Show and check:** A person-by-situation table separates comparisons. An experiment
  diagram shows controls. Sourced distributions expose overlap and uncertainty.

### Biological psychology

- **Build:** Start with a behavioral observation and a proposed biological mechanism. A
  brain measurement is not a direct reading of a thought.
- **Research:** Use a neuroscience reference for the mechanism and the original
  measurement study for the claim. Check spatial and temporal resolution and alternative
  interpretations.
- **Show and check:** A labeled anatomy diagram locates structures. A signal trace shows
  measurement scale. A study figure tests the link. Distinguish illustrative anatomy
  from observed records.

### Clinical psychology

- **Build:** Start with a clearly labeled educational case and the question a study
  addresses. Shared symptoms do not establish an individual's diagnosis or treatment.
- **Research:** Use current professional guidance and systematic reviews for educational
  claims. Check population, diagnostic criteria, study design, outcomes, and limits of
  applicability.
- **Show and check:** A dated criteria excerpt explains distinctions. A study-design
  diagram shows comparison. Outcome plots show uncertainty and applicability.

### Research methods

- **Build:** Start with a construct, a measure, and competing explanations. A reliable
  measure may lack validity. Association may reflect a confound.
- **Research:** Use a methods text plus the study protocol and analysis. Inspect
  operational definitions, reliability, validity, uncertainty, and replication.
- **Show and check:** A measurement table connects construct to observations. A causal
  diagram exposes alternatives. A sampling simulation shows uncertainty. Inspect
  replication evidence.

## Source use

Use [the source-use guide](../references/source-use.md). Catalog entries are
leads; inspect the relevant section before using it. Match the source to the
subfield and question. Start with an accessible explanation, then inspect the
technical argument or evidence needed for the agreed depth. Record the section,
its job, and any access limit in the course research notes.
