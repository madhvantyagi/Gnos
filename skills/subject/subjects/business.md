# Business

## Route by task

| Area | Inspect first | Teaching decision |
| --- | --- | --- |

## Prerequisites and distinctions

Identify the decision owner, objective, time horizon, feasible alternatives, and constraints. Separate revenue, profit, and cash; distinguish a forecast from an observed result. Avoid assuming that one company case proves a universal strategy.

## Mini lesson and evidence

Compare two product options using contribution margin, capacity, and demand assumptions. Change the binding constraint and explain whether the choice changes.

Evidence of progress is an independent explanation or solution under a changed
condition, with assumptions and limitations stated. A repeated definition alone
is not evidence of transfer.

## Handoffs

Accounting owns financial recognition; economics supports market mechanisms; psychology supports behavioral hypotheses; CS supports software operations. Business integrates these into a stated organizational decision.

## Representation profile

Start with the decision owner, alternatives, objective, constraints, and time
horizon. Use a decision table for explicit trade-offs, a process map for
operations, an organization or responsibility map for coordination, and a
financial model for cash and unit economics. Use a simulation for demand,
capacity, queues, hiring, pricing, or runway under changed assumptions. Use
animation only when work, information, or cash moves through several stages.
Keep case dates, company facts, and assumptions in text. A clean diagram does
not establish that the organization works that way.

## Source selection

Use inspected cases, company filings, operational data, and original research. Distinguish promotional claims from measured outcomes and date market assumptions.

First write what the learner must inspect, change, compare, or work out
in one sentence, and pick the smallest medium that lets them do it. Only
then check this table for what business usually needs. Never use the table
as a reason to order its favorite medium. Depth and length still cap the
media: a survey earns mostly text plus one medium.
