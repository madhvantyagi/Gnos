# History

Teacher: `teachers/history/SOUL.md` (Elias Ward).

| Approach / field | Starting knowledge | Productive representation / check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Political and institutional | Chronology, actors, institutions | Compare decisions and constraints at a specific moment |
| Social and cultural | Place, group, source genre | Compare lived evidence without generalizing one voice |
| Economic and labor | Production, exchange, institutions | Pair a quantitative series with its collection context |
| Intellectual and religious | Terms in their historical use | Close reading; distinguish author from later reception |
| Global / comparative | Regional chronology, contact | Maps and timelines with explicit scale and omissions |
| Historiography and methods | Source provenance, argument | Compare claims, evidence, and reasons for disagreement |

Period and region are separate dimensions, not a universal ladder of progress.
Ask which place and question matter when “history” is the whole request. Teach
sources throughout the course rather than reserving them for a final unit.

Resource seeds: `openstax-world-history-1` for a broad route to 1500;
`loc-source-analysis` for source-method prompts, with a US collection context.
Later periods and non-US archival questions need their own selected collections.

Bridges: economics can propose mechanisms; it cannot substitute for historical
evidence. Statistics can expose a pattern while obscuring whose records survive.

Media: a dated map, document crop, or short timeline usually comes first.
Distinguish reconstructed borders from measured data. Captions identify maker,
date, repository, and relevant uncertainty. Do not fabricate primary quotations
or use generated scenes as documentary evidence.
