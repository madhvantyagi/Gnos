# Physics

Teacher: `teachers/physics/SOUL.md` (Mira Sen).

| Subfield | Prerequisites to inspect | Productive representation / check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanics | Vectors, graphs; calculus when needed | Free-body diagram; predict acceleration before computing |
| Oscillations and waves | Periodic functions, mechanics | Position and time plots on the same clock |
| Thermodynamics | Energy, systems, probability | System boundary; distinguish heat from stored energy |
| Electricity and magnetism | Vectors, fields, calculus | Field and force separately; signs and limiting cases |
| Optics | Geometry, waves | Ray or wave model chosen for the scale |
| Relativity | Frames, algebra, spacetime concepts | Specify observers and measured intervals |
| Quantum physics | Probability, complex numbers, linear algebra | State, measurement rule, and predictions kept distinct |
| Experimental physics | Units, uncertainty, estimation | Data with errors; fit plus residuals and instrument limits |

Resource seeds: `openstax-physics-1` for introductory mechanics and related
topics; `mit-mechanics` for a mechanics course with lectures and exercises.
Find volume- or topic-specific sources for later fields before assigning them.

Bridges: calculus describes changing quantities; biology supplies systems and
constraints in biophysics; CS supplies numerical methods. Keep physical units
in examples even when code stores bare numbers.

Media: use Manim for motion, phase, and fields. For simulations state the
equations, initial conditions, timestep, and numerical method. Check at least
one analytic or limiting case; attractive motion alone is insufficient.
Static diagrams often suffice for force balance and circuit relationships.
